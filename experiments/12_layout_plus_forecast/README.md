# Study 12 — Combining the Best Action Layout with a Rain Forecast

## Research Question

Study 7 showed that giving PPO a forecast of upcoming rainfall can improve its performance, while Study 9 found that combining a four-level irrigation grid with a joint nitrogen–water action head can also help. This study investigates whether combining these two changes leads to further improvements.

The central question is whether a better action representation and additional information about future rainfall work together to help PPO outperform a tuned rule-based policy and close the remaining gap to the hindsight oracle.

## Experimental Setup

All experiments use the improved PPO configuration from Study 2, with `ent_coef = 0.003`, 10 epochs, 1 million training timesteps, and five random seeds.

The agent uses a four-level irrigation grid of `{0, 25, 50, 75}` mm and a joint nitrogen–water action head. Its observation also includes a forecast of rainfall during the upcoming fortnight. We evaluate three forecast-noise levels:

- **0 mm:** A perfect rainfall forecast.
- **10 mm:** A moderately noisy forecast.
- **25 mm:** A more uncertain forecast.

The forecast is generated by adding Gaussian noise to the upcoming fortnight's rainfall, with negative values clipped to zero. As in Study 7, the forecast is sampled once per season.

The threshold-based policy is retuned on the four-level water grid, reusing the tuning from Study 9's `fine4_joint` experiment. The hindsight oracle is recomputed for each forecast environment.

To understand the contribution of each change, we compare the combined configuration against three sets of results: the improved PPO agent without a forecast, the four-level joint-head agent without a forecast from Study 9, and the forecast-only agents from Study 7, which use the standard water grid and separate action heads.

Every configuration is evaluated on the same held-out seasons from 2017 to 2023, with 30 pest-growth replicates per season. Profit is reported in INR per hectare before environmental penalties, and ± represents the standard deviation across training seeds.

The performance gap is calculated as the agent's profit minus the corresponding reference policy's profit. A positive gap means the agent outperforms the reference, while a negative gap means it falls short. The Shapley values break down the agent's gain over `threshold_tuned` into contributions from nitrogen, water, pesticide, and sale decisions.

## Key Findings

The combined configuration performs better than the standard-layout PPO agent at all three forecast-noise levels. Its largest gain over the corresponding no-forecast layout is observed with a perfect forecast.

With a perfect forecast, the combined agent earns 88,635 INR/ha, reaching 99.1% of the oracle's profit. At 10 mm of noise, it earns 88,508 INR/ha, and at 25 mm it earns 87,405 INR/ha.

All three combined configurations outperform their grid-specific threshold baseline on average. The largest advantage is 2,090 INR/ha with a perfect forecast, followed by 1,963 INR/ha at 10 mm of noise and 861 INR/ha at 25 mm.

However, the combined configuration does not outperform the forecast-only agent when the forecast is perfect. The forecast-only agent earns 88,689 INR/ha, slightly above the combined agent's 88,635 INR/ha. This means the action-layout change does not provide an additional gain in every setting.

## Analysis

### 1. Combining the action layout with a forecast improves performance over the layout-only agent

The four-level joint-head agent without forecast information earns 86,821 INR/ha. Adding a rainfall forecast increases its average profit to:

- 88,635 INR/ha with a perfect forecast, a gain of 1,814 INR/ha.
- 88,508 INR/ha with 10 mm of noise, a gain of 1,687 INR/ha.
- 87,405 INR/ha with 25 mm of noise, a gain of 584 INR/ha.

These results show that forecast information remains useful when the agent uses the alternative action layout. The size of the improvement depends on forecast quality, with the largest gains occurring at lower noise levels.

The combined agent also remains ahead of its grid-specific threshold baseline in all three settings. Its advantage is 2,090 INR/ha with a perfect forecast, 1,963 INR/ha with 10 mm of noise, and 861 INR/ha with 25 mm of noise.

### 2. Forecast quality still matters when the action layout is improved

The combined agent's performance decreases as forecast uncertainty increases:

| Forecast noise | PPO profit (INR/ha) | Gap to oracle | Oracle performance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 mm | 88,635 ± 317 | −848 | 99.1% |
| 10 mm | 88,508 ± 81 | −975 | 98.9% |
| 25 mm | 87,405 ± 611 | −2,077 | 97.7% |

The difference between the perfect forecast and the 10 mm forecast is relatively small, at 127 INR/ha. The drop becomes much larger at 25 mm of noise, where profit is 1,230 INR/ha lower than with a perfect forecast.

This suggests that the combined action layout can make effective use of moderately noisy rainfall information, but performance becomes more sensitive when the forecast is less reliable. The experiment does not establish a precise noise threshold beyond which forecast information ceases to be beneficial.

### 3. The combined agent does not consistently outperform the forecast-only agent

Comparing the combined configuration with the standard-grid, separate-head agent from Study 7 gives a more nuanced picture.

| Forecast noise | Standard grid + forecast | Four-level joint layout + forecast | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| None | 86,200 ± 542 | 86,821 ± 315 | +621 |
| 0 mm | 88,689 ± 295 | 88,635 ± 317 | −54 |
| 10 mm | 87,972 ± 412 | 88,508 ± 81 | +536 |
| 25 mm | 86,979 ± 258 | 87,405 ± 611 | +426 |

The combined layout performs better at 10 mm and 25 mm of forecast noise, but it is 54 INR/ha below the forecast-only agent with a perfect forecast.

The 621 INR/ha difference in the no-forecast comparison also matches the improvement observed in Study 9 when moving from the standard-layout PPO agent to the four-level joint-head configuration.

Taken together, these results suggest that the alternative action layout may provide additional benefits when forecast information is imperfect. However, the pattern is not uniform, and the differences should not be interpreted as statistically established without a paired-seed analysis.

### 4. Irrigation appears to be the main source of improvement

The Shapley values indicate that the water-management contribution becomes strongly positive when the combined agent receives a rainfall forecast.

| Configuration | Nitrogen | Water | Pesticide | Sale |

| Layout only, no forecast | −341 | +636 | −17 | +154 |
| Layout + perfect forecast | −261 | +2,394 | 0 | −43 |
| Layout + 10 mm noise | −348 | +2,458 | 0 | −146 |
| Layout + 25 mm noise | −310 | +1,246 | 0 | −76 |

<text color="secondary" size="sm">Shapley contributions in INR/ha.</text>

With a perfect forecast, the water contribution is +2,394 INR/ha, compared with +636 INR/ha for the layout-only agent. At 10 mm of noise, the contribution increases to +2,458 INR/ha, before falling to +1,246 INR/ha at 25 mm.

This is consistent with the idea that rainfall forecasts help PPO make better irrigation decisions. The combined agent also uses less water than the layout-only agent: 211 mm with a perfect forecast and 212 mm with 10 mm of noise, compared with 229 mm without a forecast.

Nitrogen and sale contributions are negative in the forecast-enabled configurations, while pesticide contributions remain zero. The results therefore point to water management as the main contributor to the combined agent's advantage over its grid-specific threshold policy.

The Shapley decomposition is an attribution of the observed performance difference, rather than definitive proof of the underlying causal mechanism.

### 5. The combined agent approaches the oracle but does not reach it

With a perfect forecast, the combined agent earns 88,635 INR/ha against an oracle profit of 89,482 INR/ha. Its remaining gap is 848 INR/ha.

At 10 mm of noise, the gap increases to 975 INR/ha, and at 25 mm it reaches 2,077 INR/ha.

The perfect-forecast agent therefore achieves 99.1% of oracle profit, indicating that the combination of additional information and an alternative action representation can bring PPO close to the hindsight benchmark. However, the remaining gap shows that improved observations and action design do not completely eliminate the limitations of the learned policy.

## Conclusion

This study tests whether combining the four-level irrigation grid and joint nitrogen–water action head with a rainfall forecast produces a stronger PPO agent.

The combined approach performs well across all three forecast-noise settings. It earns 88,635 INR/ha with a perfect forecast, 88,508 INR/ha with 10 mm of noise, and 87,405 INR/ha with 25 mm of noise. Each configuration outperforms its grid-specific threshold policy on average, and the perfect-forecast agent achieves 99.1% of oracle profit.

The results also show that the benefits of combining these changes are not universal. With a perfect forecast, the combined agent performs slightly below the forecast-only agent. At 10 mm and 25 mm of noise, however, the combined configuration performs better than the forecast-only version.

The Shapley analysis and resource-use figures suggest that improved irrigation decisions account for much of the gain. Overall, **combining a better action layout with rainfall information produces a strong PPO configuration, particularly when the forecast is moderately noisy, but it does not consistently outperform the forecast-only agent**.

A useful next step is to evaluate all configurations using paired seeds and more independently sampled forecasts, then test whether the apparent benefit of the combined layout persists with rainfall forecasts generated by a realistic prediction model.
